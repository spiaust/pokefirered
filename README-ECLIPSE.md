# Pokemon Europe: Eclipse

A separate native FireRed GBA campaign in `S:/pokemon-europe-shadow`. Play
`Pokemon-Europe-Eclipse.gba` in a GBA emulator and start a NEW GAME. Original
European Tour saves are not supported by this campaign. The original folder
and its player saves are preserved.

## Starting your journey

Choose England, France or Germany, then one of that country's three starters.
Speak to Oak's aide in London, the Paris guide, or the Berlin guide to enter
Eclipse. They also resume your chapter after a blackout. Germany's story uses
a resistance double-agent cover; nationality does not determine allegiance.

The 21-chapter campaign has eight badge battles, a recurring rival who chooses
and evolves a counter-starter, Team Eclipse, WWII resistance operations, four
League council members, a champion match and credits. Six shadow partners can
be rescued and purified. The opening rival uses one level-six counter-starter
with a basic attack; later rival battles use full teams and moves. Route trainers and rival practice matches are repeatable.

Each shelter provides a nurse, mission opponent, previous/next chapter porters,
route guide, shop, rescue partner, purification keeper and storage attendant.
Every country district also offers native storage access in both eras. Catch
partners in route grass and swap teams locally. Save normally
through the Start menu. Town Map, SELECT opens chapter guidance; A shows records.

## Villain uniforms and leaders

Eclipse grunts and infiltrators wear matching charcoal uniforms with violet
panels and a silver crescent emblem. Director Veil has silver hair and a long
coat; Vex wears a technician jacket, Rime a cold-weather scout outfit, and
Boreal a fur-lined expedition coat. The wartime patrol, research warden and
laboratory director have separate muted period designs. All eight designs have
custom battle portraits and nine-frame walking sheets, connected to 43 enemy
battles and their map actors. Battle labels identify Eclipse and occupation
opponents, and the original trainer prize factors are preserved.

Preview: `graphics/eclipse_villains/preview.png`. Generated source sheets,
final prompts and conversion details are retained beside the native assets;
see `graphics/eclipse_villains/README.md`.

## Preparing for the League

Keep damaging moves on the partners you evolve with stones. Raichu will not
learn Pikachu's later Electric attacks after evolution; the Amsterdam badge's
Shock Wave TM supplies reliable Electric coverage. Give EXP. SHARE to a partner
from the Bag's Give menu to train it alongside your active battler. The item
works while held, rather than by merely carrying it in the Bag.

The Geneva route's first trainer uses Magneton and Tyranitar. Ground and
Fighting attacks make it a useful repeatable practice match after defeating
Veil. Heal between practices. The council starts with Ice and Water partners;
bring Electric coverage rather than relying on Ice Beam against Dewgong.
Shelter nurses, supplies, storage and return travel remain available between
League matches.

## Countries and time travel

The station attendant at the shelter's lower left opens a six-region destination
menu. There are 39 European destinations with their own compact city district,
landmark, present and wartime map, local trainers and services. These add 78 maps
to the campaign's 42 maps and bring the new trainer-team total to 276. Country
Town Maps show a scrollable passport with cities, landmarks and memory status.

After chapter 12 unlocks the time anchors, speak to Celebi in a country district
to switch eras in either direction. Wartime scenery and people use sepia colors.
Win that district's wartime operation, then ask its keeper to choose a refuge
garden or research archive. Returning to the present changes dialogue, ground
tiles, an eastern shortcut and an encounter slot. The choice persists and is
made once per country. Complete all 39 memories and visit Ada in the champion
shelter for the rescue-network epilogue and a one-time Master Ball.

Neutral-country historical operations are friendly keeper battles. Other
operations oppose fictional Nazi infiltrators; their presence is not a claim
that every represented city was occupied. Contemporary country names make
navigation clear; WWII-era Soviet and Yugoslav regions are discussed as such.
The story protects communities without claiming to erase real WWII history.
The USA is reserved for a later expansion.

## World Gear settings

The campaign contact gives you WORLD GEAR and a Town Map. World Gear controls
appearance and these gameplay settings at any time:

- Normal is the default. Easy lowers new campaign trainer levels by four;
  Hard raises them by five. Rival partner IVs also change with difficulty.
- Nuzlocke is optional and independent of difficulty. Only the first wild
  encounter in a European area permits capture; both country eras share an
  allowance. Rejected capture attempts explain the rule and refund the Ball.
  Fainted partners are marked retired and cannot be healed while
  enabled, including after a blackout or PC storage. Gifts and rescues are
  allowed. Disabling the mode allows healing; re-enabling retires marked
  partners again. This variant does not force nicknames or release partners.
- Visible wild encounters default ON. Native encounter weights and rates
  choose their species and level. Walking retries absent spawns every eight
  steps. Switch them OFF for classic grass encounters. Actors occupy defined
  encounter points rather than roaming; interact to start a real wild battle.

## Weather and day/night

Outdoor districts and campaign routes now have regional weather that changes
with playtime. Northern and alpine districts use snow during their precipitation
periods; other countries use rain. World Gear offers REGIONAL, CLEAR, RAIN and
SNOW, plus LOCAL, DAY and NIGHT lighting. Shelters and other indoor maps stay clear.

LOCAL uses the emulator/cartridge real-time clock. Dawn and dusk fade gradually
into blue nighttime lighting, including while the game is off. Weather continues
to use regional playtime patterns. Lighting combines with the historical sepia
palette, while menus and battles retain their normal colors. Settings survive
ordinary Save/Continue and Restore Defaults returns both controls to LOCAL/REGIONAL.
Rain retains the native rain battle effects; falling snow is visual and does
not start hail damage. Snowflakes keep respawning after their first fall.

Visible sprites cover all 62 species in the new encounter tables, using native
sprites and original small adaptations of the base's Pokemon icons.

Amsterdam's leader explains how a Belgian NIDORAN can become NIDORINA
and then evolve with a Moon Stone into a Ground-type NIDOQUEEN. Country
travel and the shared shops let you prepare a team before challenging the gym.

Berlin's leader recommends Flying or Psychic moves against her Fighting team.
The earlier Brussels routes remain available for training a Flying partner
until it learns a stronger attack.
Zurich's Veil opens with Magneton and also uses Haunter and Houndoom. Bring
Fighting or Ground coverage for Magneton and Dark coverage for Haunter.
Berlin's earned Brick Break TM can upgrade a compatible partner's Fighting
move. Zurich's Houndoom/Pidgey practice trainer is an alternative if the
Magneton/Haunter trainer overwhelms your team; train near the boss's level 37.

Lisbon's handler carries a Weezing with Explosion. Keep another trained
partner ready, or use the shared shops' Revives and Hyper Potions in a normal
run. Madrid's Earthquake TM provides strong coverage against the handler's
Magneton and Houndoom.

Prague's rescued Houndour provides Dark coverage for the wartime Psychic
opponents. Purify it, then raise it one level to evolve; Vienna's Abra/Kadabra
practice trainer is a safe way to begin catching it up. Train it further before
Geneva's Gengar. Warsaw's rescued Lapras already knows Ice Beam, which gives
you strong coverage against Dragonite. The shelters keep these rescues
available after their chapters, so you can return when you have a party slot.
Use the battle's Shift prompt to bring in the right partner as the opponent
announces its next Pokemon.

## Supplies and evolution

Every campaign shelter and country shop sells all six evolution stones at
2,100 each, plus the complete set of status cures, Max Potions and Full Restores.
Native item-use and evolution rules apply: a shadow partner refuses a stone
without consuming it, and a purified partner can evolve normally.

## Shadow partners

Six rescue rewards require a free party slot. Shadow status persists through
PC storage and saves, adds 25% outgoing and incoming base damage, blocks
evolution and gives a purple battle palette. Care adds ten friendship per
session; at 120 friendship the keeper purifies the partner and awards the
purification ribbon. Normal friendship events also help. SHADOW and HEALED
nicknames identify rescue status. Snagging and a new Shadow move type are not
part of this ruleset.

## Scandinavian remnant hunt

After the League ending, meet Ada northeast of Copenhagen's landmark. Accept
her investigation, defeat Vex in Copenhagen, Rime in Oslo and Boreal in Stockholm,
then return to Ada. Their three relays threaten the northern rescue network and
its guardians. Opponents have three, three and four shadow partners at Normal
levels 62, 64 and 66, with curated four-move sets, held items and stronger AI.
Easy lowers levels by four; Hard raises them by five without replacing movesets.

The hunt saves each victory independently and leaves the main ending complete.
Declining does not advance it. Defeated commanders cannot be farmed. Ada gives
one Shadow Ball TM; a full TM stack leaves it pending until there is room.
Completed saves retain the hunt, reward and open League/travel facilities.

## Legendary guardians

Guardians appear as native overworld Pokemon in the present-day country districts.
Their landmark signs describe the location and unlock condition. These are real
wild battles with normal catch rates and moves. Declining, fleeing or knocking
out a guardian leaves it available for another attempt; a successful catch
removes it permanently through a saved flag. Full-party catches go to the PC.
Optional Nuzlocke still uses that country's first-encounter allowance, shared
with its historical era. Repeat attempts can be caught after disabling Nuzlocke.
Celebi remains the time-travel guide so catching a guardian cannot break travel.

## Krakow sanctuary restoration

After the League, meet the guide northeast of Warsaw's landmark to travel to
Krakow. Zofia waits west of the sanctuary castle. If you have not made Poland's
past decision, return to Warsaw, travel with Celebi and help the historical
keeper protect its garden or archive before beginning restoration.

Accept Zofia's project, then donate one Escape Rope to the western repairer,
two Super Potions to the clinic keeper, and one Water Stone (garden) or Thunder
Stone (archive) to the eastern keeper. The local shop sells every material.
Each job changes the grounds and saves independently; repeat conversations
never take more supplies. Completing all three brings back Eevee for the garden
or Voltorb for the archive. You may adopt one level-30 partner, including with
Nuzlocke enabled. A full party sends it to the PC; full storage leaves adoption
pending. Return to Zofia for one Lucky Egg. A full bag leaves that reward pending.
Healing, storage, the shop and return travel remain available after completion.
The refuge's friendly partners stay visible independently of the wild toggle.

## Catching the guardians

Bring enough Balls and healing supplies: the guardians retain their native
low catch rates. Shelter and country shops now sell Timer Balls for 1,000.
They become stronger as a battle lasts; use them during a prolonged attempt,
especially against partners that know Recover. Thunder Wave can improve your odds without ongoing damage.
Use weaker attacks carefully, allowing for critical hits, and stop attacking
before a knockout. Pressure can use up move PP quickly. A full party sends a
successful catch to the PC; you do not need to empty a party slot first.
Caught guardians stay removed after saving and Continue. Normal-mode captures
of all three pre-League birds and postgame Suicune, Lugia, Ho-Oh and Mewtwo have been
verified with purchased Ultra/Timer Balls and an earned team.

## Champion rematch circuit

you accept the circuit, he gives a training kit: reusable Surf HM03, one
Thunderbolt TM24 and one Flamethrower TM35. Teach compatible partners from
the TM Case. Each gift is awarded once; if delivery fails, return to Stewart
for the remaining gifts. Players with an active circuit can also claim the kit.
Train several partners before taking on the stronger teams. Follow
his circuit card through eight leader rematches, the four council chambers and
your rival. Visit the existing mission opponents in their League shelters;
the ordinary country districts host their separate local trainers. Previous/next
porters and station travel stay open. Stewart names your next opponent and city.
Unclaimed badge TMs remain available before a leader's rematch.

Every partner has four curated moves and a held item. Easy lowers levels by four;

## Build and verification

Run `powershell -File scripts/build-windows.ps1` with the base WSL/agbcc tools.
The native build produces a 32 MiB ROM and the Eclipse release alias. Read
STORY-ECLIPSE.md for the campaign and country roster, RESEARCH-ECLIPSE.md for
repository provenance, and TESTING-ECLIPSE.md for actual validation and limits.
The districts are compact, with stylized landmarks and shared League shelters.
One Normal-mode campaign has reached the ending with earned partners. Remaining
natural legendary captures, postgame balancing, other starting routes and
broader hardware/emulator checks remain before a polished release.


## Expanded Pokédex

On a Pokémon's Dex entry, SELECT cycles INFO, LEVEL-UP, TM and HM. LEFT/RIGHT
turn move-list pages. The displayed lists read the actual game learnsets and
machine compatibility. The roster includes all 1,025 National Dex base species
through Pecharunt, with original IDs and existing save offsets preserved.
Country districts mix their original encounter tables with a fixed reserve of
partners from every generation. Additional guardian reserves unlock after the
League ending; the seven existing story guardians keep their original gates.
Visible encounters use the same reserves and the World Gear visibility toggle.
See RESEARCH-ECLIPSE.md for source credits and battle-engine adaptations.

Automatic World Gear time now reads the emulator/cartridge RTC and follows its
local clock. DAY and NIGHT provide permanent overrides. An emulator must expose
GBA RTC support for automatic time; hardware without RTC defaults to daylight.

## Custom 2026 boot screens

A custom emerald/violet European skyline and time rift now frame the larger
Pokémon logo and Eclipse title. Celebi hovers while the Start prompt blinks.
Intro credits show 2026, and native New Game / Continue still work.

## Postgame guidance

After becoming Champion, open the map and press SELECT to read the current
postgame lead. The journal tracks the Scandinavian relays, Warsaw's timeline
prerequisite, Krakow materials and rewards, each Champion circuit opponent,
and all seven guardians. Active circuit matches take priority; started
restoration work remains visible before an unstarted remnant hunt. It also
explains Ho-Oh's three-purification and Mewtwo's six-purification requirements.
The displayed rematch levels are for Normal; World Gear difficulty still applies.
All thirteen circuit rematches and the unique Master Ball now have earned-party,
native-input coverage through cold saved Continue. Broader balance work remains.

## Friendship evolutions

Raise friendship to 220, then level up. Budew and Riolu evolve during the day;
Chingling and Snom evolve at night. Buneary, Munchlax, Woobat, Swadloon and
Type: Null evolve with friendship at either time. Eevee can now become Espeon
by day or Umbreon by night, alongside its existing stone evolutions.
Evolution day runs from 06:00 through 19:59 on the local RTC. World Gear DAY
and NIGHT override this requirement, including indoors and in the past.
Everstone and the shadow state still prevent evolution. Special modern methods
remain tracked in data/eclipse-evolution-projection.json for future production.

## Move-based evolutions

Level up while the required move is in the Pokémon's current four moves:
Bonsly needs Mimic (learned at 16), Steenee needs Stomp (28), and Clobbopus
needs Taunt (35). Tangela learns Ancient Power at 24 to evolve into Tangrowth;
Yanma learns it at 33 to evolve into Yanmega. Learning the required move on
that level-up can trigger evolution immediately. Forgetting it blocks this
route until it is learned again. Everstone and shadow restrictions still apply.

## Gender-based evolutions

Female Combee evolves at level 21 into Vespiquen; female Salandit evolves at
33 into Salazzle. Males keep their original species. At level 20, female Burmy
becomes Wormadam and male Burmy becomes Mothim. The game uses the plant-coat
Wormadam model. Espurr evolves at 25 and Lechonk at 18 for either gender,
using the roster's existing Meowstic and Oinkologne base models. Separate
male/female artwork and form-specific stats remain pending. The Pokémon's
personality and gender are preserved through evolution and saving.

## Free move reminders

Scientists offer free move reminders in all 21 chapter shelters, the 39 country
plazas in both eras, and Krakow sanctuary. Look below the route guide in chapter
shelters or beside the storage attendant in country plazas. Choose a partner,
then one of its earlier level-up moves. The native menu can replace a current
move when all four slots are occupied. Eggs cannot take lessons; partners with
no missing earlier moves have nothing to recall. Lessons cost no items or money.

Piloswine can now recall Ancient Power (a level-1 lesson), then evolve into
Mamoswine on its next level-up. Reminder lessons also recover forgotten moves
needed by Bonsly, Steenee, Clobbopus, Tangela and Yanma. Everstone and shadow
restrictions still prevent evolution.

## Modern evolution stones

Shelter shops sell Shiny, Dusk, Dawn and Ice Stones for 2,100 each.

- Shiny: Togetic → Togekiss, Roselia → Roserade, Minccino → Cinccino,
  Floette → Florges.
- Dusk: Murkrow → Honchkrow, Misdreavus → Mismagius, Lampent → Chandelure,
  Doublade → Aegislash (existing shield-form model).
- Dawn: male Kirlia → Gallade; female Snorunt → Froslass.
- Ice: Eevee → Glaceon, Crabrawler → Crabominable, Cetoddle → Cetitan.
- Leaf: Eevee → Leafeon, alongside the original Leaf Stone routes.

Use a stone from the Bag on a compatible partner. A successful evolution uses
one stone. Wrong species, wrong gender, Everstone and shadow restrictions
prevent the evolution. Eevee's original three stone and two friendship paths
remain available. These items use existing colored stone icons.

## Sylveon and all eight Eevee evolutions

Eevee learns Baby-Doll Eyes at level 15, or can recall it afterward from any
free move reminder. It is a Fairy status move with priority +1 that lowers the
foe's Attack by one stage. The move menu abbreviates its name to BABYDOLLEYES.

An Eevee with at least 220 friendship evolves into Sylveon on its next level-up
if it currently knows a Fairy move. This works in either day or night and takes
priority over Espeon/Umbreon. Without a Fairy move, their day/night friendship
rules still apply. Everstone and shadow restrictions also apply to Sylveon.
All five stone alternatives remain available, completing all eight evolutions.
